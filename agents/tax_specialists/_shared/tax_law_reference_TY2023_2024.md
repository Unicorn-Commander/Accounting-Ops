## 1.3 C-Corp Filing Deadline

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URL:** <https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1120>; <https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/i1120--2023.pdf>

**Key Rules:**
- Original due date: **15th day of the 4th month** after the close of the tax year. Calendar-year corps → **April 15**.
- Automatic 6-month extension available via **Form 7004** (extends to October 15 for calendar-year corps). Extension to file ≠ extension to pay; tax must be deposited by original due date to avoid failure-to-pay penalty.
- TY 2023 original due date: April 15, 2024. Extended deadline: October 15, 2024. **Both have passed.**
- TY 2024 original due date: April 15, 2025. Extended deadline: October 15, 2025. **Both have passed.**

**Applicability Note — 1120 Preparer:** Magic Unicorn Inc. is past the extended deadline for both TY 2023 and TY 2024. Failure-to-file penalties are accruing. File as soon as returns are complete; each month the return remains unfiled adds to the penalty balance. Prompt filing stops penalty accrual and begins the statute-of-limitations clock.

## 1.4 §163(j) Business Interest Expense Limitation

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URLs:**
- IRS Q&A page: <https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/questions-and-answers-about-the-limitation-on-the-deduction-for-business-interest-expense>
- About Form 8990: <https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8990>
- IRS Priority Guidance (2023–2024 plan): confirms ongoing guidance under §163(j)

**Key Rules:**
- Deduction for net business interest expense is limited to **30% of Adjusted Taxable Income (ATI)**.
- For TY 2023 and TY 2024, ATI is computed **without** adding back depreciation, amortization, or depletion (the TCJA reverted from EBITDA-based to EBIT-based ATI starting TY 2022). This is the more restrictive rule.
- Small business exemption: average annual gross receipts ≤ $30 million (inflation-adjusted, 2024) for the prior 3 years → exempt from §163(j).
- Disallowed interest carries forward indefinitely to the next tax year.
- **[NOTE: OBBBA enacted 2025 restores EBITDA-based ATI for TY beginning after Dec 31, 2024 — does not affect TY 2023/2024 returns.]**
- File **Form 8990** to compute and report the limitation.

**Applicability Note — 1120 Preparer:** Flag any interest expense on the MU Inc. return. If gross receipts averaged under $30M over 3 years prior, the small business exemption applies and §163(j) can be ignored. If not exempt, compute Form 8990. Mark this line item for CPA review before finalizing.

## 1.5 §174 R&D Expenditure Capitalization (TCJA Rule)

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URLs:**
- IRS Notice 2023-63 (interim guidance): <https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-23-63.pdf>
- RSM FAQ on §174: <https://rsmus.com/insights/services/business-tax/faq-capitalization-and-amortization-of-r-d-costs-under-new-section-174-rules.html>
- Thomson Reuters overview: <https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/tax-and-accounting/section-174-expenditures/>

**Key Rules:**
- Effective for tax years beginning after December 31, 2021: all Specified Research or Experimental (SRE) expenditures must be **capitalized and amortized** — no current-year expensing.
- Domestic SRE: 5-year amortization (midpoint convention = 60-month period; half-year in first and last year).
- Foreign SRE: 15-year amortization.
- **H.R. 7024 (Tax Relief for American Families and Workers Act)** which would have retroactively restored expensing was **blocked by the Senate on August 1, 2024** and was NOT enacted. The TCJA capitalization rule therefore applies fully to TY 2023 and TY 2024.
- **[VERIFY BEFORE USE]** The OBBBA (2025) may have made further changes effective TY 2025+; confirm scope does not reach back to TY 2023/2024. As of knowledge cutoff it did not.

**Applicability Note — 1120 Preparer:** Magic Unicorn Inc. is a technology/AI-infrastructure business. Any software development costs, platform R&D, or experimental expenses incurred in TY 2023 and TY 2024 must be treated as §174 SRE, capitalized, and amortized over 5 years. This is a significant change from pre-2022 treatment. Flag all development cost line items for CPA review and confirm classification as §174 vs. §162 ordinary expense. IRS Notice 2023-63 is the primary guidance document.

## 1.7 Net Operating Loss (NOL) Carryforward — Post-TCJA

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URLs:**
- IRS Publication 536 (TY 2023): <https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/p536--2023.pdf>
- IRS IRM 4.11.11 (NOL procedures): <https://www.irs.gov/irm/part4/irm_04-011-011>

**Key Rules:**
- NOLs arising from tax years beginning **after December 31, 2017**: indefinite carryforward; **NO carryback** (CARES Act carryback was a temporary exception for TY 2018–2020 only).
- NOL deduction in any carryforward year is limited to **80% of taxable income** (computed without regard to the NOL deduction, §199A, or §250 GILTI deduction).
- Pre-2018 NOLs: 20-year carryforward, 2-year carryback, not subject to 80% cap.
- The 80% limitation applies for TY 2021 and later carryforward years.
- Track NOL origin year carefully — a mixture of pre-2018 and post-2017 NOLs requires separate computation.

**Applicability Note — 1120 Preparer:** If Magic Unicorn Inc. incurred losses in prior years (TY 2022, 2021, etc.), those losses may be available to offset TY 2023 or TY 2024 income, subject to the 80% limitation. Document all prior-year NOL carryforward balances and origin years before finalizing. Obtain prior-year return transcripts (or reconstructed returns) to confirm beginning-of-year NOL balances.

## 1.8 §274 Business Meals Deduction

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URLs:**
- IRS Final Regulations TD 9925: <https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/td-9925.pdf>
- IRS Notice 2018-76: <https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-18-76.pdf>
- Federal Register final rule: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2020/02/26/2020-03723/meals-and-entertainment-expenses-under-section-274>

**Key Rules:**
- **General rule (TY 2023 & TY 2024): 50% deductible** for ordinary and necessary business meals where the taxpayer or employee is present and the meal is not lavish or extravagant.
- **Entertainment expenses: 0% deductible** (TCJA eliminated entertainment deduction entirely; meals must be separately stated from entertainment to be deductible).
- The temporary **100% deduction for restaurant meals** (CARES Act provision for TY 2021–2022) **expired December 31, 2022**. It does NOT apply to TY 2023 or TY 2024.
- Employee meals for convenience of employer on business premises: 50% deductible for TY 2023/2024 (scheduled to become 0% disallowance in TY 2026 under TCJA sunset — note OBBBA may accelerate this; **[VERIFY BEFORE USE]** for TY 2025+).

**Applicability Note — 1120 Preparer:** For TY 2023 and TY 2024, apply 50% to all ordinary business meal expenses. Confirm meals are separately invoiced from entertainment. Do not apply the 100% restaurant meals deduction — that expired after TY 2022.

## 1.11 Form 4562 — Depreciation and Amortization

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URL:** <https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i4562>

**Key Rules:**
- **Bonus Depreciation Phase-Down under TCJA:**
  - TY 2023: **80%** first-year bonus depreciation for qualified property placed in service after 12/31/2022 and before 1/1/2024.

## 2.1 Form 1099-NEC — Nonemployee Compensation

**Authoritative URLs:**

**Key Rules:**

Attorney fees are always reportable even if the law firm is incorporated. The TY 2023 1099-NEC deadline was January 31, 2024 — if unfiled, late-filing penalties are accruing.

## 2.2 Form 1099-MISC — Miscellaneous Income

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URLs:**
- TY 2023 Instructions: <https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/i1099mec--2024.pdf> (combined 1099-MISC/NEC instructions)
- TY 2024 Instructions: <https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1099mec.pdf>

**Key Rules:**
- **$600 threshold** for: rents (box 1), prizes/awards (box 3), other income (box 3), medical and health care payments (box 6), crop insurance proceeds (box 9), attorney gross proceeds (box 10).

Review all rent payments, any prize or award distributions, and royalty arrangements. Note the $10 royalties threshold — this catches even small royalty payments. Attorney gross proceeds in box 10 are separate from attorney fees in 1099-NEC and both may apply to the same payee for the same year.

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024, TY 2025 (transition)

**Key Rules and Threshold History:**
| Tax Year | TPSO Threshold | Transaction Threshold | Notes |
|----------|---------------|-----------------------|-------|
| TY 2022 | $20,000 | 200 transactions | Old statutory rule; IRS delayed ARP §9674 lower threshold |

## 2.4 Attorney Fee Reporting Rule

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URL:** <https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec> (section: "Payments to attorneys")

**Key Rules:**
- **Attorney fees of $600+** paid in the course of a trade or business: report in **box 1 of Form 1099-NEC**, regardless of whether the attorney is a corporation, partnership, or individual. §6041A(a)(1).
- **Attorney gross proceeds of $600+** (e.g., settlement payments through an attorney): report in **box 10 of Form 1099-MISC**.
- The general corporate exemption from 1099 reporting does **NOT apply** to attorney fees or gross proceeds.
- Must obtain the attorney's TIN via Form W-9. Attorney must provide TIN even if a corporation — but need not certify. If TIN not provided: subject to backup withholding at 24%.

**Applicability Note — 1099 Specialist:** This is one of the most commonly missed 1099 obligations for small corporations. Review all payments to law firms and attorneys in TY 2023 and TY 2024. Confirm W-9 with EIN/TIN on file. File both 1099-NEC (fees) and 1099-MISC (gross proceeds) if both apply to the same payee.

## 2.5 Corporate Exemption from 1099 Reporting

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URL:** <https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec>; <https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099gi>

**Key Rules:**
- Payments to C-corporations and S-corporations are **generally exempt** from Form 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC reporting requirements.
- **Exceptions (ALWAYS report regardless of corporate status):**
  1. Attorney fees / gross proceeds (see §2.4)
  2. Medical and health care payments (box 6 of 1099-MISC)
  3. Substitute payments in lieu of dividends
  4. Fish purchases from individuals
- Obtain W-9 before payment to confirm payee's tax classification and EIN. If payee checks "C Corporation" or "S Corporation" on W-9, no 1099 required (except exceptions above).
- LLC classification: an LLC taxed as a C-corp or S-corp is exempt; an LLC taxed as partnership or sole proprietor requires 1099.

**Applicability Note — 1099 Specialist:** Build a vendor review checklist: (1) collect W-9 for all vendors paid $600+; (2) check box 3/4 of W-9 for corporate status; (3) flag attorneys regardless of corporate status; (4) flag any medical/healthcare payments. Missing W-9s require backup withholding at 24% — if backup withholding was not applied retroactively, note the liability.

## 2.6 W-9 and Backup Withholding

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URLs:**
- About Form W-9: <https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-9>
- Backup withholding: <https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/backup-withholding>
- Topic 307: <https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc307>

**Key Rules:**
- Obtain Form W-9 **before** making reportable payments. Request TIN, legal name, entity type, address.
- If payee fails to provide a valid TIN: withhold **24%** of all reportable payments (backup withholding rate per IRC §3406).
- Backup withholding also triggered by: IRS notice that TIN is incorrect, payee failed to certify TIN not subject to backup withholding, payee underreported prior-year interest/dividends.
- Backup-withheld amounts are deposited via EFTPS and reported on Form 945 (Annual Return of Withheld Federal Income Tax).

**Applicability Note — 1099 Specialist:** Compile a W-9 collection status for all TY 2023 and TY 2024 vendors ≥ $600. If W-9 was not collected before payment was made, document the gap; backup withholding liability may exist. Going forward, implement a W-9-before-payment policy. Late W-9 collection reduces (but does not eliminate) penalty exposure.

## 3.4 SC Failure-to-File Penalties and Interest

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URLs:**
- SCDOR Burr & Forman analysis: <https://www.burr.com/tax-law-insights/south-carolina-tax-penalties>
- SCDOR Corporate FAQs: <https://dor.sc.gov/business-income-taxes/corporate/corporate-faqs>
- SC Code Ann. §12-54-43 (penalty provisions)
- SCDOR Policy Manual Chapter 2: <https://dor.sc.gov/sites/dor/files/Documents/Policy%20Manuals/SCTIED-2025-Chapter%202-PartA.pdf>

**Key Rules:**
- **Failure-to-File Penalty:** 5% of unpaid tax per month (or fraction), maximum **25%**.
- **Failure-to-Pay Penalty:** 0.5% per month (or fraction), maximum **25%**.
- If both apply simultaneously, failure-to-file penalty is reduced by the failure-to-pay penalty amount in that month.
- **Interest on unpaid taxes:** Federal underpayment rate (IRC §§6621/6622) **plus 3 percentage points**. Interest accrues from original due date until paid.
- No minimum penalty structure for SC was found (unlike federal $525 minimum); confirm with SCDOR directly.

## 4.4 Accuracy-Related Penalty (§6662) — Detail

**Applies to:** TY 2023, TY 2024

**Authoritative URL:** <https://www.irs.gov/payments/accuracy-related-penalty>; <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6662>

**Key Rules:**
- **20% penalty** on underpayment attributable to: negligence or disregard of rules, substantial understatement of income tax, substantial valuation misstatement.
- **Substantial understatement** for C-corporations: understatement exceeds the **lesser of** (a) 10% of tax required or $10,000, or (b) $10,000,000.
- Penalty increased to **40%** for gross valuation misstatements.
- Penalty can be avoided with adequate disclosure or reasonable cause and good faith (§6664).
- FTF and accuracy-related penalties can both apply to the same underpayment — they are not mutually exclusive.

**Applicability Note — All Specialists:** The accuracy-related penalty would apply if the returns, once filed, reveal understatement of income due to negligence or improper deductions. Maintaining contemporaneous documentation (receipts, contracts, W-9s) supports a reasonable-cause defense. Flag any aggressive or uncertain tax positions for explicit CPA review.
